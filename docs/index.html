AgriWing-100 Rev D

A 1-metre blended flying wing that maps crops in RGB and near-infrared for plant-health analysis. Rev D applies what the Dart-80 build taught: the wing bending now runs through a straight carbon carry-through instead of three belly bolts, every wing part is a solid model for the slicer, and every part was checked for a real way in and for support-free printing. That audit also found and fixed eight faults that were in Rev C.

Verdict

What changed from Rev C, and why

Rev C looked finished. Checked the way the Dart-80 was checked, it had a weak wing joint, parts that could not move or be assembled, and parts that could not be printed as drawn. All of them are fixed in Rev D.

Structure

A straight carry-through spar

At 6 g ultimate (4 g limit × 1.5) and 1234 g, the Schrenk lift distribution puts 8.0 N·m of bending on the centreline and 4.1 N·m at the wing root. In Rev C that moment had to cross the centre body from one swept spar socket to the other, and the only tie across the y = 0 seam was three M3 inserts near the belly, all on the tension side with a 30 mm lever arm. That is the Dart-80's original mistake again, so it gets the Dart-80's fix: one straight load path.

  • Joiner: Ø6 solid pultruded carbon rod, 550 mm, at x = 145 mm, z = 2.3 mm. It passes through the rear centre-body pieces on two 7 mm webs per side and slides 140 mm into each wing tube.
  • Wing tube: 8×6 carbon, glued in W1 and W2 from the root to y = 438. The line was chosen where the thinnest section still leaves 1.6 mm of wall around the Ø8.1 bore.
  • Torsion: the lift line is ahead of the spar at the root; a Ø3 carbon pin at x = 60 (85 mm ahead) takes the root torque, 0.13 N·m ultimate. The magnets only hold the wing on.
  • Everything slides along y: joiner, pin, magnets and the new seam dowels all point spanwise, so a wing goes on and off in one straight move (the Dart-80 lesson about mixed directions).

Wing

Solid wing parts and a hinge that moves

Each wing part is now the outer shape minus its functional voids only: tube and pin bores, seam dowels, magnet pockets, the servo bay, the lead channel and the hinge. The slicer puts two walls around every bore, so each carbon part sits in a printed sleeve tied to the skins by the infill. Printed at 2 walls and 6% gyroid, the solid W1 weighs about the same as Rev C's ribbed shell.

The elevon hinge was rebuilt. The elevon nose is a cone around the hinge axis at the local half-thickness (4.1 mm at the root, 2.7 at the tip), running in a matching cove with 0.6 mm clearance, and the wing lip was relieved wherever the elevon passes over ±32°. The printed knuckles and the Ø1.75 filament pin are kept. The inboard elevon end is cut square to the hinge axis, so the elevon slides off its knuckles and deflects without touching the wing root.

  • Torque rod: Rev C had the horn on the inboard elevon only and the outboard elevon floating on the round pin. A Ø2 carbon rod at 80% chord now runs through both halves (bonded), so they move as one. The rod path keeps ≥0.9 mm of wall despite the 7° washout.
  • G10 horn: 1.5 mm G10 in a 1.7 mm slot, pushrod geometry unchanged. Servo margin at 25 m/s with 20° elevon is 1.6 on an ES08MD II.
  • Servo bay: servos seated 2.4 mm higher so the case clears the conformal cover; the bay end is a 45° stepped roof instead of a 35 mm bridge.

Print

Printability: support-free on a 256 mm bed

Every STL was put in its print orientation and searched for faces within 45° of horizontal facing down. Orientations were chosen per part by scoring ten candidates, then the geometry was changed where the best orientation still left a ceiling:

  • Centre-body front pieces print root-rib down; the battery rails were thin fins that became floating plates, and now have 45° flanks that double as cell cradles.
  • Rear pieces print motor-face down; the new joiner webs got a 45° V and the joiner bore a teardrop.
  • Hatch stands on its side edge (1.3 cm² of overhang instead of 232 cm² dome-down).
  • Winglets stand on the leading edge with a brim (4 cm² instead of 60 cm²).
  • Joint faces of W2 and the winglet were made exactly planar so they sit flat on the bed.

Assembly

Assembly sequence

Centre body

  1. Halves. Bolt CB_FrontRight to CB_FrontLeft and CB_RearRight to CB_RearLeft (M3 into heat-set inserts), then front to rear.
  2. Joiner. Push the Ø6 × 550 mm carbon rod through the rear pieces from one root rib to the other so 140 mm sticks out each side. Glue the Ø3 anti-rotation pins into the front pieces at x = 60.
  3. Electronics. Battery on the rail cradles against the forward stop, Pi 5 on its standoffs, cameras on the belly windows, FC, receiver beside it, ESC aft. Hatch: tongue under the hood, lower the rear onto the magnets.

Wings (each side)

  1. Tube into W1. Epoxy the 8×6 tube into W1 from the root so it ends flush at the root face.
  2. W2, then winglet. Slide W2 onto the tube with the two Ø3 dowels (all along y) and epoxy the joint face; same for the winglet at y = 440.
  3. Elevons. Bond the Ø2 torque rod through both elevon halves, glue the G10 horn in its slot, seat the elevons on the knuckles square to the hinge line, push the Ø1.75 filament pin in from the tip.
  4. Servo. Seat the servo on its lugs (M2), arm through the slot, 1.2 mm Z-bend pushrod to the horn, cover on.
  5. Mount. Slide the wing onto the joiner and pin until the magnets pull it home.
Test

Before the first flight

  • Coupons: print three 10×10×80 LW-ASA bars standing up and snap them across the layers; they must hold ≥5 MPa. Pull-test one M3 heat-set insert in a printed block; it must hold ≥150 N.
  • Proof load: support the centre body, load each wing to 4 g (≈24 N, 60% inboard of y = 300). No creak, full recovery.
  • Controls: full throw ±25° without binding, both halves of each elevon move together, zero free play.
  • CG: x = 69.5 mm, measured aft of the root quarter-chord point (x = 0 in the CAD, 95 mm behind the nose). Glide-test before powered flight.

Limits of this analysis

  • Hand analysis of beams and joints, not FEM. Foamed LW-ASA allowables are assumptions until the coupons are tested.
  • CFD is the Rev C geometry (fully turbulent, no propeller). The hinge-gap changes of Rev D are not modelled.
  • Vendor models (Raspberry Pi 5, Camera Module 3, EMAX servo, 21700 cells) are used for fit checks only and are not redistributed.
